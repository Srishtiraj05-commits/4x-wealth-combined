Bonds & Fixed Income.

Understanding income-oriented investment solutions: fixed-income securities are debt instruments through which an investor provides capital in exchange for contractual cash flows and principal repayment.

THE FIXED INCOME LIFECYCLE

How Contractual Income Works.

01

Capital

Investor provides initial capital to an issuer (Government or Corporate).

02

Debt Instrument

Issuance of specified bond, debenture, or sovereign security.

03

Contractual Cash Flows

Periodic coupon interest payments distributed as per stated terms.

04

Maturity / Exit

Repayment of principal at maturity or sale in secondary bond markets.

SOVEREIGN BACKED

Government Bonds (G-Secs)

Domestic-currency Government securities issued by the Central or State Governments (Treasury Bills, Dated G-Secs, State Development Loans).

Sovereign credit framework carrying zero credit default risk according to the RBI.
YIELD ENHANCEMENT

Corporate & High-Yield Debt

Corporate bonds, debentures, and institutional fixed deposits represent financing raised by enterprises carrying higher coupon yields.

Subject to credit rating evaluation (AAA/AA), seniority, and issuer financial health.
RISK & DILIGENCE MATRIX

What to Consider in Fixed Income.

Credit Risk

Issuer's ability to meet contractual principal and periodic coupon payment obligations.

Interest Rate Risk

Bond prices move inversely to changes in prevailing market interest rates and RBI policy repo rates.

Liquidity Risk

Ability to sell the security in secondary debt markets at or near its prevailing fair market value.

Maturity & Duration

Time remaining until contractual principal repayment and modified duration price sensitivity.

Instrument Structure

Coupon frequency, security collateral, call/put options, and seniority in liquidation order.

Reinvestment Risk

Future interest cash flows may need to be reinvested at different prevailing interest rates.

FIXED INCOME INVESTMENTS CAN DIFFER MATERIALLY IN CREDIT QUALITY, LIQUIDITY, AND TENURE.

Alternative Investment Funds (AIF).

Privately pooled investment vehicles established under the SEBI (AIF) Regulations, managed according to sophisticated investment strategies and defined terms.

SEBI INVESTOR FRAMEWORK

₹1 CRORE*

Standard minimum commitment per investor in a typical commitment-based SEBI AIF scheme. Accredited Investors may be subject to specific regulatory relaxations.

CATEGORY I

Category I AIF

Funds that invest in start-up or early stage ventures, social ventures, SMEs, infrastructure or other sectors which the government or regulators consider socially or economically desirable.

  • Venture Capital Funds (VCF)
  • Infrastructure Funds
  • Social Impact Funds
  • Special Situation Funds
CATEGORY II

Category II AIF

Funds that do not fall in Category I and III and which do not undertake leverage or borrowing other than to meet day-to-day operational requirements.

  • Private Equity (PE) Funds
  • Real Estate Funds (REIT/Debt)
  • Structured Private Debt Funds
  • Fund of Funds
CATEGORY III

Category III AIF

Funds that employ diverse or complex trading strategies and may employ leverage including through investment in listed or unlisted derivatives.

  • Long-Short Equity Hedge Funds
  • Quantitative & Algo Funds
  • Arbitrage & Derivative Strategies
  • Multi-Strategy High Yield
HOW STRUCTURES DIFFER

PMS vs AIF Comparison.

CRITERIA PORTFOLIO MANAGEMENT (PMS) ALTERNATIVE INVESTMENT FUNDS (AIF)
Management Client-specific customized portfolio management. Pooled fund structure managed according to scheme strategy.
Structure Portfolio managed for individual client under agreed mandate. Investors participate in a pooled private investment vehicle.
Min. Threshold ₹50 Lakhs ₹1 Crore
Liquidity Subject to portfolio agreement and market liquidity. Depends on scheme structure, lock-in, and tenure terms.
Documentation PMS agreement & periodic disclosure statements. Private Placement Memorandum (PPM) & contribution agreement.

AIF SCHEMES CARRY SPECIFIC RISKS INCLUDING CAPITAL LOCK-IN, ILLIQUIDITY, AND LEVERAGE RISKS.
